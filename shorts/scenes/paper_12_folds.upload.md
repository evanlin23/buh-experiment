**Description:**

```
Everyone says you can't fold paper more than 7 times. The real limit is length: every fold loses paper into the curved edge.

The maths: Britney Gallivan's formula gives the minimum strip length for n folds of paper t thick:
L = (πt/6)(2ⁿ + 4)(2ⁿ − 1)

For 0.1 mm office paper:
6 folds: 22 cm (still fits on A4)
7 folds: 88 cm (A4 runs out, the famous 7-fold limit)
10 folds: 55 m (almost the Tower of Pisa)
12 folds: 879 m (taller than the Burj Khalifa)

In January 2002, high-school student Britney Gallivan folded a 1.2 km roll of toilet paper in half 12 times.

And "fold paper 42 times to reach the Moon"? You'd need a strip about 107,000 light-years long, roughly the width of the Milky Way.

Which number surprised you most? 👇
```
