**Title:** How Long Must Paper Be To Fold It 12 Times?

**Description:**

Everyone says you can't fold paper more than 7 times. The real limit is the length of the paper. Each fold loses paper into the curved edge, so the strip has to be much longer than it looks.

The maths: Britney Gallivan's single-direction folding formula gives the minimum strip length for n folds of paper of thickness t:
L = (πt/6)(2ⁿ + 4)(2ⁿ − 1)

For 0.1 mm office paper:
- 6 folds needs 22 cm, which still fits on an A4 sheet.
- 7 folds needs 88 cm. A4 runs out, and that's the famous 7-fold limit.
- 10 folds needs 55 m, almost the height of the Leaning Tower of Pisa.
- 12 folds needs 879 m, taller than the Burj Khalifa (828 m).

In January 2002, high-school student Britney Gallivan really did it. She folded a 1.2 km roll of toilet paper in half 12 times.

And the famous "fold paper 42 times to reach the Moon"? That needs a strip about 107,000 light-years long, roughly the width of the Milky Way.
